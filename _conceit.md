# ON THE SOURCE TIERS

*An editorial note.*

This volume is assembled from paper. Every networked record in the galaxy was lost in the event catalogued here as **The Wipe**. What remains is what was printed, filed, stamped, carried, or remembered, and what could be read off damaged media by people working without funding in rooms that are frequently cold.

We are not historians. Most of us are volunteers. We hold no authority and issue no findings.

Because the quality of our sources varies enormously, every entry in this volume carries exactly one tier tag. The tag describes how we came to know the thing, not whether the thing is true. A tag is a statement about our evidence and nothing more.

**`INTACT`.** A physical record survived and was read directly. Ledgers, manifests, municipal codes, construction specifications, insurance inventories, incorporation papers. These entries are the most reliable in the volume. They are also, as a rule, the least interesting, because the documents that survive are the documents institutions took the trouble to keep in triplicate.

**`RECOVERED`.** Pulled from damaged media. Where the source is illegible we mark the gap and do not fill it. A passage reading `[...]` is a passage we could not read. It is not an abridgement.

**`TESTIMONY`.** One person remembered it and told us. We record an initial and a settlement. We do not record full names, because several of our early informants asked us not to and we saw no reason to treat the rest differently.

**`CONTESTED`.** Two or more surviving sources disagree, and we have printed them together. We do not adjudicate. Where accounts cannot both be correct we say so and stop. Readers who want a resolution will have to find one elsewhere.

**`INFERRED`.** We guessed. We have marked every place we guessed. An inference in this volume is generally an arithmetical one: a population estimated from a housing roll, a casualty figure estimated from a structure.

A note on figures. Readers will observe that this volume can state the mass of a shipment to a fraction of a tonne and cannot state how many people lived on the world it left. We are aware of this. It is a property of the surviving record and not of our method. Commercial and military accounting was filed in duplicate, insured, audited, and stored in fireproof rooms. Census material was not.

Cross-references are printed at the foot of each entry. A cross-reference marked `[ENTRY NOT RECOVERED]` points to something we know is missing. We have chosen to print these rather than remove them, on the reasoning that a reader is better served by a name and an absence than by neither.

*[Compiler's note: this volume is incomplete and will remain so. We are printing it now because several of the people who spoke to us for it have since died.]*
