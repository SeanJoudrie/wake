# Quinn — expansion ideas

NOT CANON. These are Claude's suggestions, not the author's. Nothing
here is settled and nothing here should be treated as fact. Kept
separate from design/canon for that reason.

## Off-ramps he refuses

1. Somebody clears his medical debt outright. Papers, a clean legal
   exit, a civilian life on a quiet world. He turns it down, because
   accepting the gift means conceding the debt was ever real.
2. He finds his own name in the Guard ledger and sees exactly what he
   was valued at. It is a small number. He could leave knowing. Instead
   he decides to make the number bigger.
3. Karagash asks him to stop. Once. The only person who ever does.
4. A widow of one of the Sonaff five sets him a place at her table.
   Dinner, a normal evening, an offer of somewhere to be. He eats and
   he leaves.
5. Jimzon offers him a way out, which is the only thing Jimzon has left
   to give anybody.

## How the empire actually starts

6. His first real money is not a robbery. He shorts a bounty. He knows
   a target is already dead, buys the contracts cheap off hunters who
   do not, and collects. Pure information arbitrage. Nobody is hurt and
   he makes a fortune, which is exactly why it works on him.
7. He writes insurance for bounty hunters against a job going bad.
   Everyone signs, because it is a good product. Now he holds
   everybody's downside, which means he owns their risk, which means he
   owns them.
8. He buys debt. Not people, debt. Guard service contracts sold at a
   discount, the same ninety-five per cent instrument that nearly
   killed him. He becomes the creditor of soldiers.
9. The protection racket is unbeatable because it is not a racket. It
   is actuarially sound. He can prove it on paper.
10. He fixes a bounty on an innocent to create demand, then solves it.
    First time he hurts somebody who did nothing.

## Keeping him a person and not a thesis

11. The shower. He nearly froze to death, so he takes long scalding
    ones. The first thing he buys with real money is not a ship, it is
    a water heater.
12. He keeps the books by hand forever. Refuses automation. It is the
    only thing that puts him to sleep.
13. He cannot sleep without the fluorescent hum from the accounting
    division, and has one installed wherever he lives.
14. He never learns to shoot well. That is what Karagash is for. His
    hands are for numbers.

## Cillus, afterwards

15. He takes the necklace and wears it without believing a word of it.
16. He keeps publishing under Cillus's byline. The dead journalist
    becomes his propaganda arm, which is a thing he learned from Vick.
17. He funds a shrine to Aster and never goes inside.
18. He never says the name again in any chapter, ever. The reader
    notices before anybody in the book does.

## Karagash

19. The empty bandolier fills up over the course of the books. A
    visual counter of what Quinn has become.
20. Karagash is the only one who uses his first name.
21. Eventually Quinn puts a price on Karagash too. That is the last
    line, and there is nothing on the other side of it.

## Where he crosses Jimzon

22. The big one: Quinn funds Jimzon's side without telling him.
    Jimzon's rebellion is bankrolled by the man he does not trust,
    exactly the way the Guard bankrolls the Nhath. When Jimzon finds
    out it ruins him, because it means his entire fight has been
    somebody's line item.
23. Quinn buys the Wakeful Islands. All of it, including the grave.
    Offers it to Jimzon as a gift.
24. Kelsa's child. Quinn is the one who quietly makes sure it is
    provided for, and nobody can tell whether that is love or
    acquisition, including Quinn.

## How he ends up in that armour

25. Oren was a cyberbiology scientist and Quinn watched him die first.
    The augmentations later are built out of a dead man's discipline.
26. He does not lose the limbs in a battle. He trades them. Sells parts
    of himself for leverage and gets better ones back.
27. Graves said he had seen men who were dead stand up and walk again,
    and Quinn dismissed it as the cold getting to him. Quinn becomes
    the thing he refused to believe in.

## Structure and voice

28. His chapters read colder than everyone else's. Ledger cadence.
    Numbers as section breaks.
29. Every Quinn chapter opens with a price.
30. Book one has no Quinn chapters at all, which the reader only
    registers in book two when they start. The reader missed him
    exactly the way Jimzon did.
