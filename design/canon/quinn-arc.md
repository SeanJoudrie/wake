# Quinn Domnall — the arc

Author-supplied, from voice. Recorded as given. Not in the manuscript yet.

## Order of events

1. Sonaff. He and Cillus survive it. He wakes with a chest scar and
   nothing else. Whatever costs him the body comes later.
2. They leave on a cargo ship he picked because his accounting told him
   the security rotation, and they go to a desert world.
3. Cillus decides to turn them in. Quinn shoots him.
4. He works bounties with Karagash.
5. He fixes on Jimzon and starts tracking him, across chapters nobody
   else in the book sees.
6. Third act: he finds them on Shayron and joins them.
7. He takes the Guard operative into the cistern. One occasion.
8. Jimzon decides to hand himself in. Quinn shoots the transport down.
9. He ends the book stuck with Jimzon and Kelsa.
10. The union, the private army, the trade routes and the artifacts are
    after that. The next book opens a little later than this.

## Why Jimzon

He reads the defection as a plan. A captain does not walk out without
one, and that walkout is what gave Quinn his own opening. He concludes
there is a rebellion being run, that Jimzon is running it, and that he
wants in. He is wrong about all of it and it does not matter.

## Not an interrogator

The cistern is one occasion, not a profession. He has never held the
post and does not hold it afterwards. The point is not that it is his
job. The point is how good he turns out to be at it.

## The rise

He falls in with a couple of bounty hunters. Working alongside them he
works out that they are being underpaid for the same reason everyone in
this galaxy is underpaid: they are competing against each other instead
of setting a price together. So he unionises them.

That is the seed of everything. It is not an army at first, it is a
bargaining bloc. It becomes a **de facto private military** loyal to him
personally, because he is the one who can move markets, prices and
bounty values. He controls what the work is worth, so he controls who
works.

From there:

- They take control of some trade routes.
- They run a couple of false flags. The same play the Guard runs, at a
  smaller scale.
- They sell protection. Mafia, but quiet about it.

## The method

His campaign against the Guard is financial and social before it is
military. Money, finance connections, infiltration, moles, and turning
people he judges likely to defect — on either side of the war. He is
not trying to beat the Guard in a battle. He is trying to weaken both
sides enough that a vacuum opens, and then rise into it.

Tonally: Daenerys. A liberator who accumulates.

He is also chasing artifacts, and that begins before he has the army.
The whole thing is a multi-year saga.

## Cillus

This happens **before** the private army exists.

Cillus decides to turn them in. He is about to contact the Guard and
surrender so that he can go home.

Quinn tells him he cannot do that, because they will kill Quinn.

They are romantically involved. Recently, and trauma-bonded from
Sonaff. It is subtext rather than stated.

It becomes something close to a western standoff.

Quinn shoots him. Then he cries.

He has just killed the part of himself that believes in love at all.

## After

From there he Walter Whites his way into being an evil creature. He
keeps his points — his arguments are not wrong — but he becomes a
supervillain, and a big fish.

The driver underneath all of it: he is terrified of being stepped on,
of being discarded, of being a pawn in somebody else's game.
