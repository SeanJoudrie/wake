Where the encyclopedia is thin

191 entries · 28,103 words · measured, not eyeballed

Method

Four numbers per entry. Words, body only. Inbound, how many other entries point at it. Outbound, how many it points at. Cited, whether a scene in the manuscript exists for it.

The useful metric is not length. It is inbound against words: an entry the volume leans on constantly and says little about. That ratio finds structural holes that reading cannot, because a short entry reads fine in isolation and only fails when forty other entries have promised it.

TIER ONE. Fix these first

1. The Guard is the most referenced entry in the volume and one of the least developed for its load

73 inbound references. 373 words. The next most referenced entry has 34. Nothing else is close. Seventy-three entries make a promise about this institution and it answers with a page.

Worse, seven whole systems from your own §6.1 are named nowhere in the corpus:

  • The bid system. Units apply for tasks; strong completion records win bids. Your own note calls it load-bearing: nobody assigned Castle Team their record, they chose it. One line of the-guard gestures at it. It should be its own entry, INTACT, with acceptance rates filed by unit, because the archive would have those and they would be devastating in a dry table.
  • Spec Ops as a prestige track. Re-entering the ladder at a designation that outranks it. Absent entirely. It also fixes your Staff Sergeant to Captain problem diegetically.
  • Rank structure and the unified force. A company with divisions, not an army. Two sentences exist.
  • Movement rights. You identified this as the Guard's real appeal, the thing every world actually joined for. It is one clause inside the-guard. In a galaxy that watched borders decide who lived, this deserves the same weight as the franchise.
  • The Mothership rotation as a scheduling system that governs everyone's life.
  • Contracts and completion bonuses as an employment relation.
  • The Academy wardship pipeline exists but is not connected to the recruiting incentive.

What to write: split the-guard into a shorter parent and four to six children. guard-service-contracts, the-bid-system, spec-ops, movement-rights. Each INTACT, each mostly numbers.

2. Persons is an island. Nothing outside it points at any of the eighteen

0 of 18 persons are cross-referenced from any non-person entry. They exist; nothing reaches them. The category reads as a bolt-on because structurally it is one.

What to write: this is mechanical and high value. Korjin belongs in corefuel and wake-extraction-station. Daiser belongs in indentured-servitude and council-of-riflo. Quinn and Cillus belong in moon-of-sonaff-incident, which literally says it wishes the names existed somewhere. Pavlock belongs in marfeld as the worked example of the muster roll. Liles belongs in guard-board-of-directors. Tiam belongs in academy-memorial-walls and resmole.

That is roughly twenty edits and it converts an island into the connective tissue of the volume.

3. There is no entry for the Galactic Calendar

Every world entry has a Calendars in use field. Thirty-seven worlds fill it in. The thing itself has no page. Your §2.1 calls the two-tier calendar the connective tissue of the whole wiki and the cheapest cross-link available, and it is the one system with no entry at all.

What to write: galactic-calendar, INTACT, and planetary-calendars, TESTIMONY. The pairing is the point and you already wrote the reason: the Guard files everything in Galactic; local reckonings survive only as testimony. Right now the site asserts that in thirty-seven metadata fields and never once states it.

4. The Board has seven seats and one name

guard-board-of-directors is 223 words about the body that runs the galaxy. Liles is the only member in the corpus, and he is not even an oligarch by your own design.

Your §6.1 has three more sketched: the benevolent one, the worst, the middle. None are named. This is the largest character-shaped hole in the setting, and it is not an encyclopedia problem, it is a manuscript problem the encyclopedia is exposing.

What you need to decide: names for at least the benevolent Director and the worst one. The archive can then carry them exactly as it carries Liles, which is to say as commercial interests with undeclared holdings and no recorded dissent. Their thinness would be the characterisation.

5. Bounty hunting has no entry and an entire character depends on it

Your §5.5: depression lawlessness turned law enforcement into an industry, which is how Quinn finds a whole planet of them to unionise and how Zack's family trade exists. The corpus contains nothing. No bounty-hunting, no warrant-brokerage, no licensing body.

What to write: bounty-hunting, CONTESTED or INTACT. The Guard licenses warrants, or it does not and the trade operates in the gap; either is a strong entry. It should cross-reference tongishaw (no bounty register because no authority to keep one), galactic-standard-law, and the-cartels.

6. Regenerative medicine is the thesis of your §5.3 and has no entry

space-vaccines covers prophylaxis. The finite stockpile of cure-anything medicine, the thing that determines Quinn's scars, Berji's face and Pav's hand, appears as two sentences inside another entry.

The priority schedule expressed in terms of a recipient's assessed value to the service is one of the coldest documents this archive could hold, and it is currently a subordinate clause.

What to write: regenerative-medicine, INTACT. Stock on hand, issue rates, the priority schedule quoted in its own words. Then medical-debt, because your Ch. 8 makes the Guard run indenture on its own soldiers through fees, and that connects to indentured-servitude on Maspin. Those two entries sitting three pages apart is the whole argument.

7. Custodial labour is referenced by three entries and has no page

galactic-standard-law mentions sentence extension. targath records prison labour rolls. targath-magnetic-web records ore mined by custodial labour. jax turns on it entirely. There is no entry.

What to write: custodial-labour, INTACT. Extension grounds, labour returns by installation, and the proportion of non-human persons on those rolls, which the Targath entry already implies and never states.

TIER TWO. Real holes, less urgent

8. The relay towers deserve their own entry

Faith organisations built and still own the interstellar broadcast network. Currently a paragraph in ministry-of-propaganda. Your §5.6 makes it a system: the Ministry has to negotiate with churches for airtime. relay-towers, CONTESTED, with the ownership disputed between eleven proprietors and the Guard, would carry it properly.

9. Flora is the weakest category and the least connected

17 entries, 93 words mean, median inbound 2. Six are orphans. They read as a list of nouns rather than an ecology. The strong ones prove the rule: astonian-coral works because it is doing thematic labour, floom-eggs works because it is load-bearing.

What to write: give each remaining plant or animal an economy, the same rule you set for species. Who grows it, who buys it, what it is worth, what it replaced. rockroot, spireleaf, starfruit, glazberries and yinmelon currently have none.

10. Ten world entries are missing the standard field block

actom, wedear, korno, lerroni, kinton, nancast, polaris, burial-moon, nhath-world, fawn. Six of them are Guard installations, where the omission arguably reads as deliberate. Four are not, and the inconsistency shows.

What to write: complete the blocks, or make the omission explicit with [FILING INCOMPLETE] so it reads as an absence rather than an oversight. The second option is better and cheaper.

11. Riflo: seven entries point at it, 43 words

The seat of the Council, the port of an entire world, and it is two sentences and a shipping figure. Everything about Maspin routes through it.

12. Materials has two entries and a median inbound of 17

corefuel and maldo-steel are the two most leaned-on things in the volume per entry. There is room for plasma as a material rather than a weapon, given plasma mining is one of the five industrial target categories of the war and appears nowhere.

13. Nineteen orphan entries

Nothing points at: astrovin, cargo-cults, fawn, neo-alchemy, rockroot, spireleaf, yinmelon, and all thirteen orphaned persons. cargo-cults is the worst loss. It is 199 words, it carries your thermonuclear-warhead set piece, and no entry in the volume refers to it.

What NOT to fill

Four absences are doing more work than any entry would. Protect them.

  1. queen-traceen. Three entries point at the reigning sovereign and she has no page. It is the best dead link in the volume and it is theologically exact: nobody can file a goddess as absent.
  2. the-autumnals. A stub listing one hull, one banner, one tunic. Writing their platform would destroy the entire premise.
  3. the-first-world. The Guard's own origin planet is unnamed in its own archive.
  4. nhath-disbursing-fund. The fund that paid the Nhaths, identified by internal reference only. The reader assembles this. The archive must never state it.

What I would do, in order

  1. Wire the eighteen persons into the entries that should already mention them. Half a day, and it is the difference between a bolt-on and a spine.
  2. Write galactic-calendar and planetary-calendars. Two entries that thirty-seven others already assume.
  3. Split the-guard and write the-bid-system. The single highest-leverage piece of writing available.
  4. Write regenerative-medicine and medical-debt, and place them near indentured-servitude.
  5. Name two Directors. This one is yours, not mine.
  6. Decide the seven Tier 1 planets, or decide the classification is disputed and print the dispute.
  7. Give Flora an economy each.

Items 1 to 4 and 7 I can do from what already exists. Items 5 and 6, and everything in Tier Three's first table, need you.
