# CIVILIAN DRESS
`INTACT`

Nobody here dresses like the end of the world. Everybody is a little poor, so everybody buys things that last.

**The standard set**, which you will see on any street on any member world:

Tan trousers, hard-wearing, cut much the same everywhere and stitched differently on every planet, which is how people tell at a glance where a stranger got dressed. An undershirt. A main shirt over that. And a jacket, thin or thick depending on the sky, with a great many pockets. Not a comic number of pockets. The number a working coat has when the person wearing it is carrying their own things all day. Boots, good soles, and the design of the sole is a local matter people have opinions about.

**The shirt is where the person is.** A club badge, most often a moonball side. A religious mark. The name of a town. A piece of art somebody liked enough to wear. It does the same job a shirt does now.

**Worn technology, because there is not enough of it.** Devices are scarce and expensive, so they are carried on the body rather than left anywhere: readers, lamps, meters, translators, whatever somebody has scrapped together or found second hand. None of it matches. A person's kit is a record of what they could get.

**Bags.** Satchels and packs, nearly universally. People are moving, and people who are moving carry everything they own on the strap.

**Off duty**, a soldier dresses like where he is from and not like the Guard, which is why a bar full of them looks like a bar full of eleven different worlds and not like a unit.

**And then some worlds ignore all of it.** Great volumes of fabric, pastels, garments in variety and colour that would be absurd anywhere else and are simply Tuesday there. The tan trousers are the average, and the average is not the interesting part.
