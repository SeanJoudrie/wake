# CIVILIAN DRESS
`INTACT`

Nobody here dresses like the end of the world. Everybody is a little poor, so everybody buys things that last.

**The standard set**, which you will see on any street on any member world:

Trousers, hard-wearing, cut much the same everywhere and stitched differently on every planet, which is how people tell at a glance where a stranger got dressed. An undershirt. A shirt over that. And a coat, thin or thick depending on the sky. Boots, good soles, and the design of the sole is a local matter people have opinions about.

**The pockets, and why.** Every coat has a lot of them. Chest, shoulder, a slot for a pen, the arrangement a soldier would recognise. Not a comic number. A tasteful number, and always more than you would design for one person.

Nobody chose this as a style. It is what happens when a garment has to be made in one run and sold to a farmer, a clerk and a mechanic at the same time. You cannot tool up for three coats, so you make one coat that does not fail any of them, and a coat that does not fail any of them has pockets.

Which means the most ordinary object in the galaxy quietly contains the entire manufacturing situation, and nobody wearing one has ever thought about it.

**The shirt is where the person is.** It is the only garment anybody chooses, and the reason is that it is the cheapest thing they own.

A moonball club. A university. A town, a district, a whole planet. A religious mark. A geometric pattern. A print loud enough that somebody's mother has commented on it. A piece of art somebody liked enough to wear.

Club badges turn up thousands of light years from the club, which is the one solid piece of evidence that at least some leagues are followed across worlds by people who will never attend a match.

**Worn technology, because there is not enough of it.** Devices are scarce and expensive, so they are carried on the body rather than left anywhere: readers, lamps, meters, translators, whatever somebody has scrapped together or found second hand. None of it matches. A person's kit is a record of what they could get.

**Bags.** Satchels and packs, nearly universally. People are moving, and people who are moving carry everything they own on the strap.

**Off duty**, a soldier dresses like where he is from and not like the Guard, which is why a bar full of them looks like a bar full of eleven different worlds and not like a unit.

**And then some worlds ignore all of it.** Great volumes of fabric, pastels, garments in variety and colour that would be absurd anywhere else and are simply Tuesday there.

*[Compiler's note: what is described above is a statement about what gets manufactured, not about anybody. Average the galaxy's people and you get a person who does not exist. We have printed the coat because the coat is real. The person wearing it is their own business.]*
