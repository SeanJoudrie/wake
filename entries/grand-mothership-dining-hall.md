# THE GRAND MOTHERSHIP DINING HALL
`INTACT`

Warm tans and golds, in a ship that is otherwise white and blue all the way through. It is the only room aboard that does not look like a machine.

Ornate chandeliers hang from a ceiling high enough to make the space feel like weather. The sleek white structural beams are still there, still glowing faintly with the ship's own light, but everything hung on them and set beneath them is amber, tan and gold. Long tables and deep booths run the length of it, and the noise is a constant weather system of laughter, cutlery and shared war stories.

**What is actually on the tray.** Grilled white meat, dusted with teal and red flakes. A garnish of yellow spireleaf sprouts arranged on the side by somebody who was told to arrange it. Circular tan grains, soft and steaming. A tall glass of opaque pastel-yellow chai.

It is good. That is the surprising part. The bread comes out of the ovens hot enough to smell from the elevator, and the spices are real, carried aboard from a dozen worlds by people who missed them. A man can come off six weeks of ration bars, walk through those doors, and be undone by the smell before he has even sat down.

It seats the entire embarked complement in three sittings, and it cost more to fit out than the medical wing, which is in the accounts and has never been commented on.

You are required to come out of armour to eat. The standing order gives the reason as morale, and it is correct: it is the one hour of the day when a unit stops looking like a unit and starts looking like five people who know each other.
