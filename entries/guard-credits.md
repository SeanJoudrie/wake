# GUARD CREDITS
`INTACT`

The money. A credit is a line in the Guard's ledger, and it is worth something because the Guard says it is.

Nobody outside the accounting division calls them credits. On a dock or in a bar it is Guardcoin, said the way you say the name of a company that owns your town.

**And then there is the coin.** Struck silver, heavier in the hand than anybody expects the first time, in the same denomination as the ledger and carrying none of its properties. You can put a few of them on a table without a single system anywhere knowing that you did.

That is not a survival from the paper decade. It is current issue, and it is the only part of this arrangement the Guard has never explained in writing. A ledger entry has a sender, a receiver, a date and a reference. A silver credit has a weight.

**Which is the whole of it.** The Guard built a currency that records everything, and also mints the exception. Every bounty settled on a fringe world, every retained item under Standing Order 41 that somebody afterwards turned into money, and a great deal of what moves through the cartels, moves in metal, because metal does not reconcile.

When the Wipe took every financial record in existence, money went back to paper for about a decade while the accounts were retyped, and then the credit replaced it. It is not backed by metal or grain or anything else. It is backed by the Guard's word and by the fact that member worlds collect tax in it.

**Where the ledger works.** Wherever Guardnet reaches. Beyond that you settle in paper and hope somebody reconciles it later, at the discretion of whoever you are dealing with.

Which means the answer to whether your savings exist depends on where you are standing. Cut a world off the network and everything its people own becomes a rumour until it comes back.

The ledger rules allow settlement to a world or a district to be suspended by administrative decision. It has happened nineteen times in twenty years, for an average of forty-one days.
