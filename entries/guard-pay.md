# GUARD PAY
`INTACT`

It is a very good living, and almost none of it arrives while you are alive to spend it.

**The structure.** A contract runs a fixed term and pays 95 per cent of the total on completion, as a single lump sum. In the meantime you draw 1.3 per cent a year.

The stated justification is that the Guard already provides everything: your quarters, your meals, your medical care, your safety. You have nothing to spend it on, so it is held for you. The real reason is that the Guard is the only employer in the galaxy worth the name, and nobody has anywhere else to go.

**What that looks like in hand.** A Spec Ops captain's contract is worth about 110,000 credits a year, untaxed, because no member world has the standing to assess a Guard contract and the Guard does not assess itself. Over a full term that is a fortune. What actually reaches him each year, before completion, is roughly the price of a good coat.

So the money is real, and it is also a rumour. It exists as a number in the accounting division, and it becomes yours on one specific day and not a day earlier.

**What is not held back.** Whatever you retain at a cleared objective under Standing Order 41 is yours immediately, is entered nowhere, and men at that level do not lose much of what they pick up. The Guard is entirely comfortable with this. A soldier with a bag of somebody else's gold is a soldier who is not asking about his balance, and a soldier holding stolen property is a soldier who cannot easily complain about anything.

**What it looks like from the other side of the ledger.** A single Spec Ops operation ends a criminal system, or takes a cartel principal, or hands control of a whole sector to somebody who will pay for it. The Guard books that against 110,000 a year, most of which it is still holding.

A million credits over a decade, against what a decade of that produces, is not generosity. It is the best purchase in the accounts.

**And the term that follows from it.** Note what the payment schedule rewards. The contract costs the Guard almost nothing until the day it ends, and everything on the day it ends. Which means the cheapest possible outcome, for a man in his final months, is one where the day never comes.

Nothing about that is personal. It is arithmetic, done on paper, by people who have never met him. That is the part that should worry you.
