# GUARD RANK STRUCTURE
`INTACT`

An ordinary army ladder, with two things bolted onto it that are not ordinary at all.

Privates at the bottom, sergeants in the middle doing the actual work, captains and colonels above them, and the Board of Directors at the top where the ladder stops being a ladder and becomes a company.

**The ship exception.** Command a vessel of a certain class and you may be rated a Commodore, which sounds naval and grand and sits at roughly the same height as a captain of infantry. It exists because the Guard absorbed several fleets that already had the word and found it cheaper to keep it than to argue.

**The exception that matters.** You can leave the ladder and re-enter it at the bottom.

A soldier accepted into special operations comes in as a Special Operations Private. It is the lowest rank in that arm and everybody starts there, whatever they were before.

And a Special Operations Private outranks a captain.

**What that actually buys.** Not the right to do as you please. Something narrower and stranger: nobody stops you. You are not asked where you are going, or why you are on that deck, or what is in the case. Doors that require a reason for other people do not require one from you. It is a privileged freedom rather than an unlimited one, and it is the single most valuable thing the Guard gives anybody, because it cannot be bought and it does not appear on any pay schedule.

Which is why a promotion to captain, for a man already inside special operations, is a smaller event than it looks from outside.
