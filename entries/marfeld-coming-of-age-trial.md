# THE MARFELD COMING-OF-AGE TRIAL
`TESTIMONY`

The rite by which a Marfeld boy becomes a man and acquires standing. Universal on the world, including for the children of off-world residents.

**Form.** At seventeen the boy is put out into the wild with his clothing and nothing else, for a period of weeks. He is not told in advance what conditions he will face, which season he will be given, or by which of three paths he will go.

**The three paths.** Blood, the hunt. Root, the forage. Sky, the climb. Catalogued separately. The paths correspond to the directions one would range from a settlement and to the three ways a person can be fed by that country.

**Origin.** The rite was originally the first hunting season, undertaken from necessity, with a hunting group, for months, to bring back meat. Its present form retains the exposure and has removed the group.

**The rule of silence.** No man who completes the trial may speak of it. The rule is absolute, is observed, and extends to speaking of it with other men who have completed it.

The Restoration has interviewed forty-one Marfeld men. Every one declined. We do not press and have instructed our volunteers not to.

**What is publicly known.** The season varies. Summer brings heat, insect burden and injury to the feet. Winter brings storms, and the accounts that circulate on the world, told by people who did not undertake the trial, describe boys crossing frozen rivers on strips of bark and sewing hides to keep their toes.

**Note.** The Restoration observes that a rite of this form, in its original version, would have carried a casualty rate, and that a rule forbidding a man to speak of what happened is consistent with a rule forbidding him to say who did not come back. This is inference and is marked as such. No informant has confirmed it and none has denied it.
