# PASHMIRSKÉ
*the mark is written by some people and not by others, in the same office, in the same year*
`RECOVERED`

Fought over for a hundred and fifty years by two powers that did not want it, and quiet now.

**System:** Pashmirské (single star, nine bodies, one habitable)
**Status:** independent. No accession, no protectorate order, no garrison, no consulate
**Population:** not established. The last figure anybody filed was filed by a sponsor, for its own purposes, and is a count of a district
**Membership:** not a question that is asked here
**Calendars in use:** the local reckoning. Galactic Standard appears in the port and nowhere behind it
**Principal exports:** magnetic ore, biodium

**The war was already here.** That is the part outsiders get wrong. Nobody arrived and started it. There were factions on this world with grievances of their own, generations deep, and what the two powers did was notice them and pay.

Neither sponsor ever fought here under its own colours. They wrote cheques, shipped crates, and let the argument that was already running do the work.

**What was used on it.** Rifles and ordnance. Men walking onto ground and taking it a street at a time, and then losing it, and then taking it again. Nothing on this world was ever worth a strategic weapon, and both sponsors understood that perfectly well, which is why the damage here is not a crater. It is a hundred and fifty years of small holes in everything.

**Why it went on so long.** Because neither sponsor needed to win it. A war on Pashmirské cost one of them very little and cost the other a great deal, and the accounting worked out the same in both directions, so both kept paying. On the sponsors' books this world appears as a line item that performed well.

**How it stopped.** Not with a victory, a treaty or a surrender.

The two powers paying for it destroyed each other. The money stopped. And in a galaxy that cannot manufacture, the resupply stopping is not an inconvenience: it is the end. There is a bottom to a war fought with weapons nobody can make another of, and after sixty more years of grinding toward it, they reached it.

The fighting did not conclude. It ran out.

**And somebody was still standing.** Whoever holds a capital on the day a war finally exhausts itself holds the world, and what followed was not a campaign. It was a consolidation, conducted mostly on paper, against factions that had no ammunition and no patron and nothing left to be promised.

**Now.** Quiet, orderly, poor and fed. There is one authority and it does not pretend to be anything else.

**The ore.** The world holds magnetic mineral of the grade used in plasma weapon manufacture. It is the only export of any consequence besides the fuel, and there is no longer anywhere to send it, because nobody has been able to build a plasma weapon since the Wipe. The deposit is worked anyway and the material is stockpiled and sold in small quantities against a demand that does not currently exist.

**Biodium.** The second export and the one that actually pays. It is a combustible extracted from the residue of a rainforest that dried out on this world long before anybody arrived on it, and it runs domestic and light industrial plant. It flies nothing. It is catalogued separately.

**Scrap.** A century and a half of ordnance and vehicles is in the ground here, and in an age that cannot manufacture, that is stock rather than wreckage. `INFERRED`

**The Guard.** There has been no approach in either direction. No application, no refusal, no incident, no complaint. The two parties have arrived at an arrangement that neither has written down and both observe, which is that they do not have anything to do with one another.

**The mark on the name.** Some filings carry it. Some do not. The same office does it both ways inside a single year and nobody local treats this as a matter to be settled.

*[Compiler's note: we hold a great deal about this world, and every page of it was produced by somebody who was paying for the fighting. Requisitions, sortie returns, insurance schedules, casualty estimates prepared for a sponsor. What we hold about the sixty years since is four pages. The war is documented because outsiders were doing the accounting. The peace is not, because the man running it does not file.]*
