# THE POLARIS SYSTEM
*also known as: the breadbasket*
`INTACT`

Ten inhabited bodies running one machine with the parts on separate worlds. Most of what the member worlds eat comes out of here.

**Composition:** two planets, Kelton and Weton, and eight worked moons
**Status:** Guard administration seated on Kelton. Outer bodies keep local government. Licensing, banking and the harvest ledger do not
**Population:** between 200 million and 900 million across all ten
**Membership:** application, and assessment. There are more applicants than places in every year returned
**Calendars in use:** the Polaris Harvest Reckoning, shared across all ten because the logistics demand it. Galactic Calendar for external contracts only
**Principal exports:** grain, flour, oil, protein, meat, salt, seed, drink

**How it fits together.** Weton grows the grain. Dorrow mills it. Sheel raises the meat and, out of the same yards, renders the bone and blood the Weton soil runs on. Corr ships the water. Schriggé ships the salt. Tspa grows the protein. Tall holds the seed. Baline holds the cold. Kelton holds the paperwork.

Break any one of those and the system stops. Most people in it can name three.

**What you get.** A house, a guaranteed price for your crop, a wage floor, a rest day, and a repair entitlement that means when the combine breaks it gets fixed. We have compared these terms against forty-one other agricultural worlds. Nothing comes close. Farmers move here on purpose, and apply, and wait.

**Clause 11.** Leaving the system requires an approved leave filing that states a date of return. It sits at clause eleven of the standard residence instrument and the port authority on Kelton enforces it. Filings are approved 96.8 per cent of the time.

**The scrip.** Wages are paid in Polaris scrip, which is excellent money and is not money anywhere else. Conversion to Guard credits happens at the Kelton exchange, subject to an annual per-person limit that 0.4 per cent of holders reach.

You can leave. You cannot take it with you.

**The libraries.** Funded from the harvest levy and unusually good: a mean of 41,000 volumes a settlement against a member-world mean of 9,200. A great deal of it is about economics, which is to say about yield, supply, and the dignity of feeding people.
