# PRE-WAR LANGUAGE FAMILIES
`INFERRED`

Before the war, humans were organised above the level of the planet. Several large cultures held territory across many worlds at once, each with its own language, and several of them had done so for a thousand years or more.

They fought each other regularly and none of them was destroyed by it. What destroyed them was the war that ended everything else: the supply chains stopped, the trade stopped, the worlds stopped being able to reach one another, and a culture that cannot move is a culture that becomes several. Within two generations there were no multi-world human polities left. There were planets, some of which joined the Guard and some of which did not.

The languages did not die. They stopped being spoken above the planet, which for a language spread over eleven worlds amounts to the same thing.

**What survives.** Not literature and not speech. Suffixes, attached to names, carried by people who have no idea what they are carrying. The pattern is visible in the shipping registers because the registers list every world at once, and it is visible almost nowhere else.

**Kanta.** The language of the human homeworld, and the oldest attested human tongue. It marked descent: a name in *-zin*, *-zon*, *-jin* or *-in* meant the son of somebody.

Kanta had longer than anything else to erode and it shows. The ending is still doing its work in some places, where *Ninzin*, *Jayzin* and *Iradin* are family names passed down. Elsewhere it has come loose entirely and become a given name meaning nothing at all. *Jimzon* is a man's first name and it means the son of Jim. His father was called *Korjin*, which means the son of Kor. Neither was named after the other and neither has been told.

The homeworld is a debris field. Kanta is the only one of these families whose speakers are actually gone, and it is the most widely distributed of the three, because the culture that spread furthest was not the one that survived.

**Myatugo.** The farming language. Its mark is *-ton* and *-tom*, and it meant a holding: ground under somebody's charge, entered in a book.

It sits on **Kelton**, **Weton** and **Kinton**. Two of those are the planets of the breadbasket and the third is a fruit world four systems away with no connection to either. All three grow food. Nobody on any of them regards the other two as related.

**Saolai.** The administrative language, and the one that carried law, accounting, scholarship and the ceremonial of courts. Its mark is a final *-o*, *-a* or *-ia*.

It sits on **Maldo**, **Creta**, **Katanzaria**, **Kitalia**, **Saigo**, **Korno** and **Riflo**, and what those worlds have in common is not geography. Maldo is a Royal administrative capital. Creta is where the galaxy banks. Katanzaria keeps the most complete civil registry in this volume, running eleven centuries. Saigo is the chartered university world.

The worlds carrying the clerical suffix are the worlds that still keep records.

**The others.** There were more than three and some of them were larger. Their marks are on the register too, in *-ar* and *-or* and *-vor*, in *-eld*, in *-ow*, and on two worlds whose names still carry a diacritic that Galactic Standard has no use for and that half of all filings simply drop.

Which cultures those were is not established here. The material to establish it would be their own writing, and their own writing did not survive the thing that ended them.

**The collision.** Myatugo *-ton*, a holding, and Kanta *-zon*, a son, were never related and have worn toward one another until they cannot be told apart. **Penston**, **Armstin** and **Krostin** could be either. Nobody can say whether they name a piece of ground or a man's father.

The homeworld's older name is **Waketin**, which carries both.

**And the leaks.** *Penston* is a person wearing a ground ending. *Astrovin* is a wine wearing a descent ending. Neither is an error. Languages in contact borrow from each other constantly and get it wrong just as often, and a system with no exceptions in it would be evidence of a forgery rather than a history.
