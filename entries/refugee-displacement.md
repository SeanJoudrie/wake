# THE REFUGEE DISPLACEMENT
`INFERRED`

Population movement during and after the Great War. The largest movement of persons in the recorded period, and the least documented event in this volume relative to its scale.

**Scale.** Not established. Estimates in circulation range from 2 billion to 11 billion persons displaced. The Restoration's own inference, from housing stock returns, ration issue where filed, and port arrival counts on 41 worlds, falls between 4 billion and 9 billion. We publish the range because publishing a figure would misrepresent it.

**Conditions.** The receiving worlds could not manufacture. Housing, water, medicine and food could be moved and could not be made. A receiving world of 400 million taking a billion arrivals was not making a policy decision. It was making an arithmetical one.

**Recorded responses, by category, from the surviving municipal and port record:**

- Admission without limit, 9 worlds
- Admission with containment in camps, 22 worlds
- Admission by selection against stated criteria, 31 worlds
- Refusal of admission, 19 worlds
- Refusal of admission enforced by fire on shipping, 6 worlds

Each of these categories is a category of document. The port authorities filed their orders and the orders survived.

**Consequence.** Populations moved along survival lines and not along alignment. The recorded effect is that most worlds finished the movement hosting substantial populations from the other side of the war, and that internal conflict on many worlds preceded any declaration.

**Descendants.** The status of persons arrived during the displacement, and of their descendants, is a live question in the statute of 44 worlds in this archive. In 12 of them it remains unresolved in the third generation.

**Note.** Free movement of persons between member worlds is the term of Guard membership cited first in nine of the eleven accession debates this archive holds in full.
