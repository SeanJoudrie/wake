# SANSVOR
*said quickly by anyone from there, the name comes out in about a syllable and a half*
`CONTESTED`

A rich world that stopped being maintained. Almost nothing here was destroyed. Almost nothing here has been repaired either, and the difference between those two sentences is the whole of the place.

**System:** Sansvor (single star, ten bodies, one habitable)
**Status:** independent. No garrison. Guard consulate since GC 4433
**Population:** between 100 million and 600 million
**Membership:** residence. There is no instrument and no authority empowered to issue one
**Calendars in use:** Galactic Calendar in the ports; at least four local reckonings in daily use, none official
**Principal exports:** none of consequence. Scholarship, salvage, and people

**What they built.** The century before the war was an inventive one here and they spent it: airship terminals, rail, cut stone, cathedrals, civic halls, libraries with reading rooms sixty metres long. Then the paint ran out, and nobody made more paint, and a wall came down and there were no stonecutters left.

The characteristic building on Sansvor is structurally sound and open to the sky.

**Why that is survivable.** The weather is mild. A classroom missing a wall and half a ceiling is still a classroom here, and is used as one. Desks, board, books, and you keep your coat on. Birds come in. Nobody stops the lesson.

Nothing in the country is dangerous. Prey animals, and a raptor that takes them.

**Who is still here.** The universities did not close. Their endowments failed, the faculties went unpaid, and a good number of them kept teaching anyway. Literacy returns, where a local authority still bothers to produce one, are among the highest in this archive. The museums are intact and uncatalogued. Dragon's blood trees have come up through the roof of the north rail terminal and are now part of the roof.

**Politics.** Between nineteen and thirty organised associations of any weight, the range being a disagreement about what counts. Read their literature and you find the argument is about what the world used to be and who gets to say what it becomes next. It is not about food. Nobody here is short of food.

**The blockade,** and the reason this entry is filed as contested.

Guard material, and most people in the port towns, hold that a belligerent closed the world's shipping during the war and the Guard broke it open in GC 4409, and that the Guard is therefore remembered here as the people who came.

A smaller number, mostly inland, hold that the closing continued after the war ended, and continued deliberately, until the world could be relied on to ask.
