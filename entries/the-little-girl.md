# THE LITTLE GIRL IN THE WAITING ROOM
`TESTIMONY`

A child in the medbay at Base Delta, with a puzzle in her lap, waiting for her father.

She looks up at whoever comes in, blinks, tilts her head, and crosses the room with the kind of confidence only children and fools have. Then she sits down opposite you, uninvited, and starts.

> *"Are you a clown?"*

She is a hard audience. Told a joke, she does not laugh. Shown a costume flower that honks twice, she does not laugh at that either. Her mouth holds a perfect little frown.

Then she explains why she is there.

> *"My teacher told me my daddy lost his leg. He isn't here yet. My teacher said he walked on a mine."*
> *"Mommy was too."*

Her father is a Trailblazer who stepped on a mine. Her mother was one.

> *"Daddy really likes his legs. I think he's probably sad now."*

She will laugh, eventually, at a bad enough joke. How much do rainbows weigh? Not much, they are actually pretty light.

> *"I will, mister. You're a funny clown."*

We do not have her name. We are not looking for it.
