# THE MARRIAGE BRAID
`TESTIMONY`

You do not buy it. You grow it.

**The rite.** On marriage, a small braid is started in the hair and is never cut for as long as the marriage lasts. It can be hidden, tucked, kept tiny, or worn out where everybody can see it, and there is enormous variation in how: burned at the ends, dyed, beaded, wrapped in thread. What is constant is that it is braided extremely tight, and that keeping it is your job.

A neglected braid is a public statement, and everybody reads it as one.

**Why it beats a ring.** A ring tells you somebody is married. A braid tells you how long, because it has been growing the whole time. You can read a marriage across a room, in a market, at forty paces, without asking a single question.

**The cut.** Divorce is a cut. You sever the braid, you bring it to the town council, they run a fast confirmation that the hair is yours, and it is over. That is the entire procedure. It takes an afternoon. It can be done in a temper, at midnight, by somebody who will feel differently on Thursday.

**And you may never marry that person again.** One cut is permanent. You can marry somebody else, as many times as you like. Never them.

Which produces a specific and entirely avoidable category of person: somebody who did a five-minute thing in a bad hour, twenty years ago, and has had to live inside the consequence ever since. Every world that keeps this rite has them. Everybody knows one.

**On a death**, you cut it, and you keep it. What is done with it afterward varies and informants become reluctant when asked.

**The forgeries.** The confirmation step exists for a reason, and the reason is that somebody, at some point, arrived at a council with somebody else's hair.
