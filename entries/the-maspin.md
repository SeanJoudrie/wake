# THE MASPIN
`TESTIMONY`

Bodies of smoke and vapour, about the height of a human child. Where the eyes should be there is water, held somehow, and it moves. Inside the head there is an ember, and it brightens.

They have no mouths. The voice arrives anyway, from somewhere further in.

**Faces.** The features shift with feeling, continuously, the way a fire does. Other peoples report that you learn to read this with practice and cannot read it at all before. Every human informant who has spent a season among them says the same thing: it is not that Maspin are hard to read, it is that they cannot stop being read.

**Sparks.** Snap the fingers and they come, enough to light a cigar. This is universal, nobody here finds it worth mentioning, and every visitor mentions it.

**Eating.** Mostly they do not. They take byproduct from the plants around them the way other people take oxygen, with rather more water vapour in it. What they do eat is liquid or close to it: fruit pulped and watered and drunk, absorbed into the smoke of the body rather than swallowed.

Nothing in the diet is chewed. Everything is fermented, pickled, pulped or brewed.

**Family.** They have families and it is not the arrangement other peoples mean by the word. Pressed on it they say they are one family, all of them, and appear to mean it literally rather than warmly. We have asked in several ways. We are recording the ambiguity because we were not able to resolve it, and because the people we asked did not think it was the interesting question.

**Dying.** They hold that they are made from the ocean under the crust and that they go back into it. What that looks like in practice is catalogued under the Elder Tree, and it is not a metaphor.

**Work.** Fruit, kraskor, floom husbandry, port labour, temple and civic service, and the professions. Among our informants were a chemist, a caretaker, a councillor, and a clerk who keeps the tide tables at Riflo port and asked us twice whether we needed anything else.

**Status.** Indenture is lawful on Maspin and is administered by the Council. A number of the people in this entry are serving one.
