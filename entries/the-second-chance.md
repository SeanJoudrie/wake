# THE SECOND CHANCE
*not an official term. It is taken from what is said at the end of it*
`TESTIMONY`

The procedure applied to political dissent on Pashmirské. It is presented publicly as execution and is not one.

**Announcement.** The charge is read out publicly. The person is then taken from where they are, with a bag over the head, and does not return. From the point of view of anybody who knows them, they have been disappeared following a public accusation, which is the outcome the announcement is designed to produce.

**Transport.** The prisoner is driven to a remote location. Accounts differ on distance and on duration and agree that neither is explained at the time.

**The staging.** The prisoner is placed and is able to hear, but not see, what follows. There are gunshots. There is the sound of bodies falling. This is repeated. Accounts state that the sequence is unhurried and that the intervals are the worst part of it.

The prisoner is then asked whether they have any last words. Every account holds that the question is put seriously and that the answer is listened to.

**The end of it.** The prisoner is touched once on the forehead, with a finger, and told that they have been given a second chance, and asked what they intend to do with it.

The answer given at that moment does not affect the outcome. Informants who gave defiant answers and informants who begged were both released.

**Deportation.** The released person is given a ticket offworld and told what it costs. The sum is small. It is worked off in a week at a rail station, after which the ticket is issued and they travel.

They may not return. No mechanism exists for appealing this and none has been recorded as attempted.

**Who is not released.** Persons convicted of violent crime are not put through the procedure and are not deported. The distinction is drawn by the administration and is not published.

**Why any of this is known.** Everybody it has been done to is alive, offworld, and under no obligation to Pashmirské. The Restoration has recorded eleven such accounts on four worlds. They agree with one another in sequence and in wording, including on the finger and on the question, and none of the eleven was aware that the others existed.
