# THE SEED POOL
`TESTIMONY`

The couple stands in the water. The guests finish the marriage.

**The rite.** A shallow above-ground pool, waist-height and wide, built for this and nothing else. The couple stands in it together. Every guest throws in seed and crop: grain, pulses, fruit, whatever that world grows.

The marriage is complete the instant the last seed lands. Not a word is spoken. No officiant is involved. **The community physically has to finish it**, which means everybody can see who threw and who did not.

A person standing at the back with a full hand is not a private disagreement. It is an event, and it will be discussed for years.

**What you throw says who you are.** Seed off your own fields is the standard and the safe answer. Somebody who cannot manage that brings something else, and it is noticed, and most people are decent about it. A wealthy family throwing something imported reads as showing off, and is meant to.

**The image.** By the end the couple is standing waist-deep in other people's harvest, unable to move properly, laughing, being buried by everyone they know.

**And afterward, the water.** It is drained onto a single plot. What comes up is a chaotic, unplanned, mixed patch of everything forty families grow, and it keeps coming up for years, and everybody in the district knows whose wedding it was.

A marker you can point at from the road. It outlasts a ring by a considerable margin.
