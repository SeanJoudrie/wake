# THE TIPSITTERS
`INTACT`

A band, and the reason every karaoke night on every mothership eventually arrives at the same song.

Their sound starts the same way every time. A kick drum alone. Then a slow bassline underneath it. Then a crash of cymbals, and only then the lead guitar, which is the part everybody in the room has been waiting for and which everybody in the room hums along to badly.

**"Dark Holiday."** The one everyone knows. It opens sadder than its reputation, all cold air and chills that are not from the rain, and then it detonates into a chorus about not waiting for the calendar to tell you when to be alive.

> *There's a first time for everything.*
> *Sometimes we forget there's also a last.*

Then the shouted bridge, which nobody sings so much as yells: *break the glass, take the stairs, nobody really cares.* And then a spoken verse near the end, delivered in a lower register, which turns out on inspection to be sung from the point of view of death itself, patient and unbothered, waiting in the back of your mind. Most people who love the song have never noticed this.

**What it is actually for.** Soldiers. Not because it is about soldiers, but because it is about a specific arithmetic that soldiers do without being taught: that the festival might not come, so this ordinary Tuesday will have to be the festival instead.

**Other music.** The older ranks lean toward Backwater country, which is slow, sincere, and about places nobody in the room has been. The pre-war instrumentals still turn up on record players in colony houses, warm and simple, from back when things were easier. And on Maspin they sing into a hole in a tree, which is a different thing entirely.
