# TIAM ROCKSTEAD TINOPPEL
`INTACT`

Sixteen. Killed on Resmole. Somebody's first friend.

**Where they met.** The Scout Corps, the summer programme for cadets with nowhere to go between years. He was the one who wrote a letter every single night, sealed it properly, and put it in a box nobody ever came to collect.

He stole protein wafers out of the commissary, which are dry and almost flavourless and which he handed out behind the latrine wall to four other children, eaten in silence, terrified somebody would hear the wrappers. He swapped a drill sergeant's whistle for one he carved out of a bar of soap. The whole platoon ran extra laps for that and he never confessed, and he smiled every lap of the way. *Pressure makes diamonds*, he used to say, about nothing in particular.

**How it happened.** They were on the gate on Resmole, sixteen years old and bored, sharing a greyleaf cigarette. A round took out a potted plant on a roof three storeys up and neither of them looked. Then he thought he saw something move on the ridge, and he went up the clock tower to look, because he had a scope and a reason and nobody stopped him.

One shot. No ambush, no raid, no follow-up. Nobody even raised the alert.

They were not trained for that. They were children playing at soldiers, and the honest answer is that it was nobody's fault, which is the least useful thing anybody can be told.

**Afterwards.** His name is on the Academy memorial walls, on the plate for that year, in gold leaf, in order of loss, alongside everyone else who died in a sector nobody was calling dangerous.
